About

Writing a Unix shell, the classic low-level way: start with raw fork() and build up to a full shell that waits for its children.

What you'll learn

  • What fork() really does: two processes from one call
  • Reading the fork topology from a parent/child printout
  • wait() and running commands to completion

Core idea

The tutorial starts with bare fork(): one call, two printed identities - the parent sees the child's PID, the child sees zero. From there, wait() joins children back.

#include <stdlib.h>
#include <unistd.h>

Why it matters

fork() is the single most important syscall idea in Unix - understanding it at the PID level is the gateway to everything a shell does.
