About

This tutorial recreates the iconic "Upside Down" portal effect that went viral in AR demos: through a doorway framed in the real world, you see an entirely different rendered scene as if it existed on the other side. The trick combines AR plane placement, a second virtual camera and a render texture.

What you'll learn

  • Building a portal frame out of planes and setting up AR tracking.
  • Creating a second camera that renders a separate virtual scene.
  • Using a RenderTexture as the portal "window".
  • Aligning the virtual camera with the real camera for a seamless illusion.

Core idea

The portal is not magic — it is a camera pointed at another scene, whose output is drawn onto the portal's surface instead of the normal shader.

C#// Render the second camera's view into a texture and use it on the portal
portalCamera.targetTexture = portalRT;   // the portal's RenderTexture
portalMaterial.mainTexture = portalRT;   // the "other world" view on the doorway

Why it matters

The portal effect is the archetypal "wow" AR demo, but beneath it lies a genuinely transferable technique — render-to-texture — used everywhere from mirrors to security cameras in games and VR. This tutorial makes that technique tangible and fun.
