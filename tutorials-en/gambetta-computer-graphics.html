Computer Graphics from scratch

About

"Computer Graphics from scratch" (part of the "from scratch" book series) is a complete introduction to rendering written in pseudocode with JavaScript implementations. It walks through the entire pipeline twice: first a raytracer, then a rasterizer, covering canvas basics, 3D representation, lighting, textures and shading.

What you'll learn

  • Canvas geometry and basic 2D operations like PutPixel.
  • Ray tracing: ray-sphere intersection and the quadratic formula.
  • Light sources: point lights, diffuse and specular components.
  • Shadows, reflections and the recursive nature of raytracing.
  • Rasterization, perspective projection and z-buffering.
  • Textures and interpolation across triangles.

Core idea

The first raytracer render is only a handful of lines: for each pixel, build a ray from the camera, test it against the sphere, and color the pixel by the closest hit.

pseudocode// Renders the canvas, assuming the viewport is on the Z axis
for y in [-Cw by 40, Cw by 40]:
    for x in [-Ch/2 by 40, Ch/2 by 40]:
        // Build a ray from the camera through the canvas point
        direction = normalized(Point(x, y, 1) - Camera)
        t = IntersectRaySphere(Camera, direction, sphere)   // pick the smallest t
        if t is not infinity:
            color = sphere.color                            // color the pixel
        else:
            color = BACKGROUND_COLOR
        canvas.PutPixel(x, y, color)

Why it matters

The pseudocode-first approach means the book teaches ideas, not language quirks — and the same algorithms translate trivially to any language you already know. It is the rare textbook that is both rigorous enough for the math and readable enough to finish in a weekend.
