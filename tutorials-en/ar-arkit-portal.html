How to Augmented Reality AR Tutorial: ARKit Portal to the Upside Down

About

This is the original viral "portal to the Upside Down" demo for iOS: you place a doorway in the room and through it you see the eerie Stranger Things world. The tutorial builds it with ARKit and SceneKit, structured around a main SCNNode that holds the frame, the world inside — plus the crucial "camera inside camera" trick.

What you'll learn

  • Placing a SceneKit portal node in the ARKit world.
  • Building the frame with simple box geometry.
  • Filling the portal with an interior scene and its own lighting.
  • The nested-camera technique that makes the illusion hold as you move.

Core idea

The portal starts life as nothing more than a box node positioned in the world:

C#// Add a simple portal to the scene in ARKit with SceneKit
var portal = new SCNNode {
    Geometry = SCNBox.Create(1f, 2f, 0.05f, 0)
};
portal.Position = new SCNVector3(0, 1, -2);
sceneView.Scene.RootNode.AddChildNode(portal);   // camera-in-camera added later

The magic comes later: a second camera attached to the first renders the inner world exactly aligned with your device's view, so walking around the portal reveals its content from every angle.

Why it matters

Few demos teach as much AR craft in one shot: node hierarchies, coordinate systems, render alignment and camera control all show up. It is also simply the most impressive AR trick to show friends — and to reuse in your own apps.
