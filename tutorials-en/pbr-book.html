Physically Based Rendering:From Theory To Implementation

Original source: "Physically Based Rendering:From Theory To Implementation" (PBRT) by Matt Pharr, Wenzel Jakob and Greg Humphreys — the definitive textbook on path tracing.

About

PBRT is the reference book of physically based rendering. Instead of describing algorithms abstractly, it presents a complete, modern path-tracing renderer in C++ — every chapter adds a new component of the system, and the full source accompanies the text. It is widely used as the standard text in university graphics courses.

What you'll learn

  • Geometry, transforms and rays — the core data structures of a renderer.
  • Cameras, image sampling and the reconstruction of pixel values.
  • BRDFs and light transport: the rendering equation and its terms.
  • Monte Carlo integration and importance sampling.
  • Path tracing, direct/indirect lighting and global illumination.
  • Materials, textures, volumes and integrators in a real codebase.

Core idea

At the center sits the rendering equation — outgoing radiance at a point equals emitted light plus the integral of incoming light, weighted by the surface's BRDF. Everything else in the book is machinery to evaluate this equation efficiently and accurately.

mathLo(p, ωo) = Le(p, ωo) + ∫S² f(p, ωo, ωi) Li(p, ωi) |cos θi| dωi

And its practical loop — for each light, test occlusion, then accumulate the BRDF-weighted incoming radiance:

pseudofor each light source:
    if the light is not occluded:
        incident_light = light.L(point)              // light incoming at the point
        amount_reflected = surface.BRDF(hit_point,   // how much is reflected back
                                        camera_vector, light_vector)
        L += amount_reflected * incident_light       // accumulate outgoing radiance

Why it matters

If you want to understand how modern movie and product rendering actually works — including why terms like "Monte Carlo" and "BRDF" show up everywhere — PBRT is the one complete journey. It is deep, but it is also self-contained: by the last chapter you have shipped a real renderer.
