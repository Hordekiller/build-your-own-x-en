Writing a Bencode Parser

⚙ Language: Nim 📁 BitTorrent Client 🌐 English page 🔗 Original source
Original source: "Writing a Bencode Parser" — day 2 of the NimDays series by xmonader (Ahmed Soliman), building a bencode encoder/decoder in Nim.

About

Bencode is the serialization format behind BitTorrent — compact, unambiguous and easy to hand-parse. This NimDays article implements a complete bencode library in Nim: a variant-based data model, an encoder and a decoder, exercised against real-world samples. It is part of a series where each day is one small, self-contained project.

What you'll learn

  • The four bencode types: strings, integers, lists and dictionaries.
  • Representing them in a statically typed language with a variant object.
  • Writing an encoder and a decoder that round-trip correctly.
  • Handling edge cases like nested structures and raw byte strings.
  • Testing the library against known bencoded values.

Core idea

The whole format rests on one discriminated union. Everything else — encode, decode — is a recursive walk over this type:

nimtype
    BencodeKind* = enum
        btString, btInt, btList, btDict

With an encoder and decoder in place, real torrent data parses cleanly:

nimlet encoder = newEncoder()
let decoder = newDecoder()

let btListSample1 = @[BencodeType(kind:btInt, i:1), BencodeType(kind:btString, s:"hi") ]
var btDictSample1 = initOrderedTable[BencodeType, BencodeType]()
btDictSample1[BencodeType(kind:btString, s:"name")] = BencodeType(kind:btString, s:"dmdm")
btDictSample1[BencodeType(kind:btString, s:"lang")] = BencodeType(kind:btString, s:"nim")

Why it matters

Parsers are the foundation of every network protocol, and bencode is one of the simplest realistic examples — enough to be interesting, small enough to finish in an evening. It also makes a great first Nim project, showing off the language's variants, iterators and test tooling.
