Let's Build a Compiler

Original source: "Let's Build a Compiler"

About

In September 1988 Jack Crenshaw built a compiler from scratch in 16 parts for RISC magazine - still the best "first compiler" thanks to single-pass techniques and plainly explained decisions.

What you'll learn

  • The heart: GetChar, Look (current char) and Match() - all grammar rules built on them
  • Direct 68k assembly generation: D0 holds the result, D1 the second operand
  • The progression: calculator, multiplication/precedence, variables, control flow, functions, parameters

Core idea

The single-pass philosophy (no AST) keeps every part immediately understandable and directly emits assembly: Expression calls Term while Look is + or -, and Add emits ADD D0,D1 via Match.

Pascalprocedure Expression;
begin
  Term;
  while Look in ['+', '-'] do begin
    case Look of
      '+': Add;
      '-': Subtract;
    end;
  end;
end;

procedure Add;
begin
  Match('+');
  Term;
  EmitLn('ADD D0,D1');
end;

procedure Subtract;
begin
  Match('-');
  Term;
  EmitLn('SUB D0,D1');
  EmitLn('NEG D0');
end;

Why it matters

An unmatched historical and educational resource: even without Pascal, the C (lotabout) and Haskell (g-ford) ports exist; the grammar-to-assembly intuition you gain is priceless.
