About

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is the dominant pattern for building LLM assistants over your own documents: chunk the documents, embed them into a vector index, retrieve the most relevant pieces for a question, and feed them to the model as context. This LangChain series teaches every stage with runnable notebooks — from chunking and embeddings to evaluation and advanced routing.

What you'll learn

  • Document loading, chunking and text splitters.
  • Embeddings and vector stores (FAISS, Chroma).
  • Retrieval strategies: similarity search, MMR and self-querying.
  • Synthesis: prompt construction with retrieved context.
  • Conversational RAG and memory.
  • Evaluation of retrieval and generation quality.

Core idea

The entire pattern fits in a few lines once the parts exist — split, embed, retrieve, generate:

pythonfrom langchain_text_splitters import RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter

# 1) Index: split documents and build vector embeddings
chunks = RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter(chunk_size=500).split_documents(docs)
index = FAISS.from_documents(chunks, embeddings)

# 2) Retrieve: the documents closest to the user's question
hits = index.similarity_search("user question", k=4)

# 3) Generate: give the retrieved documents to the model as context
answer = llm.invoke(f"Answer based on these documents:\n{hits}")

Why it matters

RAG is how most production LLM applications ground their answers in real data. The series is practical from notebook one, and it demystifies the pipeline that powers chatbots, document Q&A and AI search products.
